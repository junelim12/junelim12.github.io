01 · The problem

Seoul's best salons are booked in Korean, on KakaoTalk

Tourists come to Seoul for hair, nails, makeup and photo shoots. The shops they want are often small, owner-run and booked through KakaoTalk or Naver. Without a Korean phone setup or the language, a visitor can find the work on Instagram and still have no way to book it.

02 · Version one

A full booking platform for tourists

The first plan was a two-sided platform: Pinterest-style browsing with the trust of a beauty review app. Artists upload their portfolios, tourists browse by taste, and each booking is routed to the channel the shop already uses, Naver Booking or a KakaoTalk channel, to keep the barrier low at launch.

Early on, bookings would run on messages: translate the tourist's request, deliver it to the owner on KakaoTalk or SMS, and nudge the owner to reply on the platform.

03 · The pivot

Too heavy for the people it served

Talking with 20+ owners of lash salons, photo studios and similar shops changed the plan. They didn't want another channel that only added ad costs to the routes they already used. Many were already handling foreign customers with Papago and other tools they knew. And with LLMs, the language barrier was no longer something a whole platform had to solve.

So I kept the one part that mattered and dropped the rest.

04 · Version two

An agent that takes bookings for you

The lighter version drops into the messengers shops already run. An agent sits in the middle, translates both ways and takes bookings from global customers on the owner's behalf.

05 · The decision

Why I stopped

The business didn't add up, so I stopped before spending more.

1

Costs vs. willingness to pay

Token and build costs outran what owners would pay.

2

Traffic wasn't enough

Owners would pay for proven revenue lift. New global traffic alone didn't justify it.

3

No defensible edge

Against incumbents, I found no edge I could defend.
