01 · Where it started

Behind the camera

Since 2020 I've run OWOL Snap as a working photographer, shooting actor profiles, album covers and portraits. A real share of my clients flew in from abroad. On those shoots I worked side by side with makeup artists and other beauty freelancers, and I kept hearing the same thing from both sides of the chair.

Artists and small shopsUnless you're a franchise with a system, handling and selling to foreign customers is really hard.

02 · The insight

Their hands are never free

The language gap wasn't only at booking. It peaked during the work itself, when the artist needs to ask how short, how bold, which angle.

What I saw

Mid-service, nobody can type

A makeup artist holding a brush or a photographer holding a camera can't stop to type into a translator app.

Why existing tools failed

OS translators drown in noise

Built-in interpreters like Apple's pick up too much background sound in a busy studio or salon to be useful.

In tech
Speech interpretation fine-tuned for beauty and photo sessions, with a human in the loop
For humans
The artist keeps working, and you still get exactly the look you asked for.

06 · The decision

Why I stopped

Version two was cheap to build, which was also its problem.

1

Free alternatives were too strong

LLMs, Papago and the translation now built into messengers made interpretation nearly free. Owners already had something good enough.

2

Global customers felt like a burden

My target was small owners who aren't fluent in English. For many of them, more foreign customers meant more work and more stress, not just more revenue.

Remove the language barrier

Interpreters and tuned AI

Costs too much to sell

or
Keep the barrier, add convenience

A translation agent

Not enough to sell

Removing the barrier cost too much. Keeping it made the product too thin to sell. So I closed the business.
